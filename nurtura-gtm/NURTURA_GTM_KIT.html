01

Executive summary

Nurtura keeps everyone caring for a loved one on the same page — so no dose is missed, no question goes unasked, and no one carries the load alone.

Caring for an aging parent, a recovering partner, or a child with ongoing needs is rarely one person's job, yet the information usually lives in one person's head. Nurtura centralizes the daily work of care: it tracks medications, dosages, and reminders as its everyday anchor, gives the whole care circle a shared view, and links to MyChart so the family can message doctors without leaving the app. The positioning is deliberately calm and practical — a dependable tool families reach for every day, not another alarm competing for attention. Premium members can add Ivy, an AI assistant that helps keep the care routine organized.

02

Who we serve

Nurtura is bought and championed by the person coordinating care, and made worthwhile by everyone they bring in.

Primary · the organizer

The family caregiver

Often the "sandwich generation" adult child — working, raising kids, and managing a parent's care at the same time.

Who they are
The default coordinator. They hold the med list, the appointment calendar, and the mental weight of what happens if something slips.
Their pain
Fragmentation and fear. Details live across texts, sticky notes, and memory. Siblings are out of the loop, a missed dose feels like a personal failure, and reaching the doctor means a phone queue.
What Nurtura gives them
One shared source of truth for meds and reminders, a care circle that can actually help, and a direct line to the care team via MyChart — plus Ivy on Premium to keep it all organized.
Secondary · the circle

The care team & the loved one

Siblings and relatives, paid or professional caregivers, and the person receiving care themselves.

Who they are
Everyone else who shows up: a brother across the country, a home aide on a shift, and the loved one who wants to keep their own independence and dignity.
Their pain
Being out of sync. They don't know what was already done, what's due next, or who to ask — so care gets doubled, dropped, or second-guessed.
What Nurtura gives them
Shared visibility into the same plan, clear reminders for their part, and a calm way to stay involved without a flurry of group texts.

Value proposition & key messages

Five message pillars. The med anchor leads; peace of mind is the feeling we sell.

Everyone on the same page

One shared care plan for the whole circle. Family and the care team see the same meds, the same schedule, and the same updates — no more "did someone already do this?"

Never miss a dose

Medications, dosages, and reminders are the daily anchor. Nurtura makes the right dose at the right time the easy, obvious thing — for whoever is on duty.

A direct line to the doctor

With MyChart linked, the care circle can message the doctor's office from inside the care routine — questions and answers land where the rest of the plan already lives.

An always-on helperPremium

Ivy, Nurtura's AI assistant for Premium members, helps caregivers stay organized — answering questions about how to use the app and the care plan, surfacing what's coming up, and summarizing the day. It supports coordination, not medical decisions.

Peace of mind

The real product is relief. When the plan is shared and the reminders are handled, caregivers can stop holding it all in their head — and get back to simply being family.

Launch plan by stage

Three stages, each with one job: build a warm list, land a credible release, then turn early relief into retention and referrals.

1
Pre-launch
Build a warm waitlist
  • Waitlist landing page aimed at family caregivers, capturing email and the care situation.
  • TestFlight beta with a small cohort of early-access caregivers for real med-tracking feedback.
  • Recruit through caregiver communities — recovery, aging-parent, and condition-specific groups.
  • Found-story content: why Bon Air Media built Nurtura, to seed trust before day one.
  • Line up Premium/Ivy as a clearly labeled beta so expectations stay honest.
2
Launch
Land a credible release
  • App Store release with the listing copy below and screenshots that lead with the med anchor.
  • Convert the waitlist first — invite in cohorts so onboarding and support stay personal.
  • PR to caregiving & local RVA press, plus senior-care and family-health outlets.
  • Partnership pilots with a clinic or aging-in-place org to reach caregivers in context.
  • Ask for the first review right after a caregiver's first "that was easy" moment.
3
Post-launch
Retain, review, refer
  • Retention loops: reliable reminders and a weekly care summary that pull people back daily.
  • Reviews & ratings prompted at genuine wins, not on a timer.
  • Referrals built in: inviting another family member is the core growth act.
  • Premium conversion: introduce Ivy once a caregiver feels the daily value of Free.
  • Close the loop with clinic partners on outcomes like adherence and fewer missed messages.

Onboarding & subscriber conversion

A screen-by-screen first-run flow built on proven subscription-app patterns: lead with value, earn small commitments, get to a first win fast, then ask for the review and introduce Premium when perceived value is at its peak. Every lever is used to support the caregiver, not to trap them.

1
Welcome, value first
Emotional promise · no signup wall
Lead with the outcome
  • Warm full-screen open: "You don't have to carry this alone."
  • One line on what Nurtura does, the care-circle mark, a single Get started button.
  • No account or payment yet — show the promise before asking for anything.
2
Who are you caring for?
Make it personal
Commitment & consistency
  • Pick a relationship — parent, partner, child, myself, someone else — and a name or nickname.
  • Framed as "so Nurtura fits your situation," not as data collection.
  • Each small answer is a step taken, which makes finishing feel natural.
3
Their care & your role
Build their plan, together
The IKEA effect
  • Light, skippable questions: care focus or conditions, who else helps, your role.
  • Answers assemble a starter care plan so the app already feels like theirs.
  • People value what they help build — investment now drives retention later.
4
You're in good company
Lower anxiety before any ask
Social proof & trust
  • Two short caregiver testimonials and the Built for trust privacy badge.
  • A real "families coordinating care" count once there is one — never a fabricated number.
  • Reassurance lands before the first-win step, so the ask feels safe.
5
Your first win
Activation / the aha moment
Small win · variable reward
  • Guide to one concrete win: add the first medication and immediately see the first reminder scheduled — or invite one care-team member.
  • Pre-fill from step 3 so the win takes seconds.
  • Celebrate it: a warm confirmation and "That's one thing off your plate," with visible progress.
6
The review ask — at the high point
Right after the first win, never on launch
Ask when they feel good
  • Trigger on a genuine positive moment: first med added or first invite accepted.
  • Lightweight in-app check first: "Enjoying Nurtura so far?" — happy or not yet.
  • Happy → the native SKStoreReviewController prompt. Not yet → a private feedback form, routed away from the store.
7
Meet Ivy · the paywall
Premium at peak perceived value
Offer help when help is felt
  • Placed right after activation, when the value of Nurtura is fresh.
  • Present Ivy as always-on help; soft free-trial framing, cancel-anytime stated plainly.
  • Anchor annual (best value) vs monthly — price TBD, shown without invented figures.
  • An easy, honest decline path; Free stays fully usable, so the choice never feels coerced.

Review prompt accuracy: ask early, but right after the first win — not on the first screen.

Apple's SKStoreReviewController is rate-limited (about three prompts per user per year) and the system shows it only at its discretion, so each prompt is precious. Firing it cold on first launch is against the spirit of Apple's guidelines and tends to draw low ratings from users who haven't felt any value yet. The proven pattern is to gate with the lightweight "Enjoying Nurtura?" happy / not-yet check and route only happy users to the store prompt — this maximizes 5-star reviews while staying within the guidelines. "Early, but right after the first win" is the sweet spot.

Ethics as a trust advantage: every technique here is meant to support the caregiver — no fake urgency, no countdown timers, no hidden cancel, no guilt for choosing Free. For an app holding health information, refusing dark patterns is itself a retention and trust advantage, and testimonials and user counts must be real, never fabricated. Ties to KPIs: the first-win step feeds activation (first med added / first member invited); the Ivy screen feeds Free→Premium conversion; and the gated prompt feeds review prompt → rating rate.

Trust, privacy & compliance

A caregiving app handles health information, so trust isn't a feature to bolt on — it's the whole reason a family lets Nurtura in. Treat it as a go-to-market advantage, and be honest about what's done versus in progress.

HIPAA & health data

Handling health information and sharing it across a care circle implicates HIPAA and state privacy law. Build the consent and data-handling model with counsel before scaling, and don't claim certification you don't have.

Consent & data sharing

The loved one's information is being shared with others, so consent flows must be explicit: who is invited, what they can see, and the ability to revoke access at any time.

Data minimization

Collect only what the care routine needs, keep it only as long as it's useful, and make roles and permissions easy to understand. Less data held is less risk carried.

Encryption

Encrypt health data in transit and at rest as table stakes, with sensible authentication and access controls across every member of the care circle.

MyChart via Epic — a process, not a plug-in

MyChart messaging runs through Epic's integration program (App Orchard / Connection Hub). It requires application, review, and approval — plan the timeline and roadmap it honestly rather than promising it as already live everywhere.

A clear privacy policy & Ivy's limits

Publish a plain-language privacy policy covering what's collected, who sees it, and how Ivy uses data. Ivy helps organize caregiving — it does not give medical advice or diagnoses, and the app should say so where people meet it.

Honest status: Nurtura is an iOS app with a SwiftUI rebuild in progress. Lead with "built for trust" as a promise backed by real steps — consent, minimization, encryption, a published policy, and the Epic process — not as a finished compliance badge.
